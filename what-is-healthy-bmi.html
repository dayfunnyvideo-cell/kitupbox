What is a Healthy BMI? (And How to Calculate It)

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple number that helps you understand if your weight is in proportion to your height. It is widely used by doctors and health professionals as a quick screening tool.

Understanding BMI Categories

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), BMI is categorized as follows:

  • Below 18.5: Underweight
  • 18.5 – 24.9: Normal (Healthy Weight)
  • 25.0 – 29.9: Overweight
  • 30.0 and Above: Obese

How to Calculate Your BMI

The formula for BMI is simple:

BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ Height² (meters)

For example, if you weigh 70 kg and are 1.75 meters tall:
BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.8 (Normal Weight)

Why is BMI Important?

Knowing your BMI can help you understand potential health risks. Being underweight or overweight can increase the risk of certain health conditions. However, BMI is not a perfect measure—it doesn't distinguish between muscle and fat. Athletes may have a high BMI but very low body fat.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

BMI is a general screening tool. It may not be accurate for pregnant women, athletes, or the elderly.

What should I do if my BMI is high?

If your BMI falls in the overweight or obese category, it is best to consult a doctor or nutritionist for personalized advice.

Does BMI differ by gender?

The standard BMI categories are the same for men and women, though body composition varies.
