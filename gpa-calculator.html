What is GPA?

GPA stands for grade point average. It turns the letter grades you earn in school into one number, usually on a scale from 0.0 to 4.0. Schools, colleges and scholarship programs use it to see how a student is doing overall. This calculator works out your GPA for a term and, if you give your earlier GPA, your new overall GPA.

How to use the GPA calculator

  1. For each course, pick the grade you got or expect to get.
  2. Enter the credits for the course. A course with more credits counts more.
  3. Add or remove rows with the buttons. You can add a course name if you like.
  4. If your school uses weighted grades, tick the weighted box and choose Regular, Honors or AP / IB for each course.
  5. To see your overall GPA, enter your current GPA and the credits you have so far.
  6. Press Calculate GPA.

GPA formula

GPA = total quality points ÷ total credits

The quality points for a course are its grade points multiplied by its credits. The usual grade points on the 4.0 scale are below. Rows with no grade are left out of the calculation.

Worked example

Say you take four courses: Math, 4 credits, grade A (4.0); English, 3 credits, B+ (3.3); History, 3 credits, B (3.0); and Chemistry, 4 credits, A- (3.7).

  • Math: 4 × 4.0 = 16.0 quality points
  • English: 3 × 3.3 = 9.9
  • History: 3 × 3.0 = 9.0
  • Chemistry: 4 × 3.7 = 14.8

The total is 49.7 quality points over 14 credits, so the GPA is 49.7 ÷ 14 = 3.55.

Now suppose you already had a 3.2 GPA over 30 credits. Your earlier quality points are 3.2 × 30 = 96. Your new overall GPA is (96 + 49.7) ÷ (30 + 14) = 145.7 ÷ 44 = 3.31.

Weighted GPA

Some high schools give extra points for harder courses, so a student can go above 4.0. A common pattern is +0.5 for Honors and +1.0 for AP or IB, which turns an A in an AP course into 5.0. For example, an AP course with an A (5.0) for 4 credits, an Honors course with a B (3.5) for 3 credits and a regular course with an A- (3.7) for 3 credits gives (20 + 10.5 + 11.1) ÷ 10 = 4.16. The extra points are added only to passing grades. Schools set their own weights, so check your school's policy.

Things to keep in mind

  • Schools do not all use the same scale. Some do not use plus and minus grades, and a few count an A+ as 4.3.
  • Colleges often work out GPA differently from high schools, and many show both a term GPA and a cumulative GPA.
  • Pass and fail courses are usually not counted. Leave the grade blank on those rows.
  • If a number matters for an application or a scholarship, use the figure on your official transcript.

Results are estimates for planning. Your school's official GPA can differ because of its own scale, rounding and rules for repeated courses.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my GPA?
Multiply each course grade point by its credits, add the results, and divide by the total credits. This calculator does it for you when you pick grades and credits.
What is a good GPA?
It depends on your goals and your school. A 4.0 is the top of the unweighted scale. Programs and scholarships set their own requirements, so check the ones you are aiming for.
What is the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?
Unweighted GPA counts every course the same on a 4.0 scale. Weighted GPA adds extra points for harder courses such as Honors, AP and IB, so it can go above 4.0.
How do I find my overall GPA with a new semester?
Enter your current GPA and the credits you have so far in the optional boxes. The calculator combines them with the new courses to give your new overall GPA.
Do credits matter?
Yes. A course with more credits counts more. An A in a 4 credit course raises your GPA more than an A in a 1 credit course.
How do I handle pass/fail courses?
Pass and fail courses are usually left out of GPA. Leave the grade blank on that row and it will not be counted.
Can I use this for college and high school?
Yes, if your school uses the standard 4.0 scale with plus and minus grades. If your school uses a different scale, the result may not match.
Is my information saved?
No. The calculation runs in your browser when you press the button, and this page does not save what you enter.
