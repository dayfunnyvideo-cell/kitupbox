What is an auto loan?

An auto loan is money you borrow to buy a car, truck or SUV. You repay it in equal monthly payments over a set number of months, usually 36 to 84. The vehicle itself is the security for the loan, so the lender can take it back if you stop paying.

What affects your monthly car payment

  • Vehicle price: the higher the price, the larger the loan.
  • Down payment and trade-in: every dollar you put down is a dollar you do not borrow or pay interest on.
  • Interest rate: set by the lender. It often depends on your credit score, the loan term and whether the car is new or used.
  • Loan term: a longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises the total interest.
  • Sales tax: added by most states. Some states tax the full price and others tax the price after a trade-in.

How to use the auto loan calculator

  1. Enter the vehicle price.
  2. Enter your down payment and the value of any car you are trading in.
  3. Enter the interest rate and choose the loan term in months.
  4. Enter your state's sales tax rate.
  5. Press Calculate Payment to see the monthly payment, loan amount, tax and total interest.

Auto loan formula

The payment uses the standard loan formula:

M = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

  • P is the loan amount: price minus trade-in, plus sales tax, minus down payment.
  • r is the monthly interest rate: the yearly rate divided by 12, then by 100.
  • n is the number of monthly payments.

Worked example

Say you buy a $30,000 vehicle with $3,000 down, no trade-in, 6% sales tax and a 7% rate. The tax is $1,800, so the loan is $28,800. Over 60 months the payment is about $570 a month and the total interest is about $5,416. Over 72 months the payment drops to about $491 a month, but the total interest rises to about $6,553.

Tips for a smaller car loan

  • Save a bigger down payment before you buy.
  • Get loan quotes from a bank, a credit union and the dealer, then compare the rates.
  • Pick the shortest term whose payment fits your budget comfortably.
  • Remember insurance, fuel and repairs. They are not part of the loan payment.

Results are estimates for planning. Your lender may add fees, title and registration costs, or use a different way to count interest. Ask for the official loan terms before you sign.

Frequently asked questions

How is a car payment calculated?
The loan amount, interest rate and number of months go into the loan formula shown above. The result is a fixed payment that repays the loan and its interest by the last month.
Is a longer car loan a good idea?
A longer loan lowers the monthly payment but you pay more interest overall, and you may owe more than the car is worth for longer. Choose the shortest term you can afford.
Does a trade-in lower my payment?
Yes. The trade-in value reduces the price you finance. In many states it also reduces the amount that sales tax is charged on, but rules differ by state.
Why is my dealer's payment different?
Dealers may include fees, add-ons, warranties or a different tax amount. Ask for a full breakdown of the price and the loan terms.
Does this include insurance and fuel?
No. Insurance, fuel, repairs and registration are separate costs. Plan for them on top of the loan payment.
Is the calculation done on my device?
Yes. The calculation runs in your browser when you press Calculate Payment.
