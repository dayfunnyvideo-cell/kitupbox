What is an EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the fixed amount you pay your lender every month until a loan is fully repaid. Each EMI has two parts: the interest charged on the balance you still owe, and a portion that repays the principal. In the early months most of the EMI goes toward interest. As the balance falls, the principal part grows and the interest part shrinks.

This EMI calculator works for home loans, car loans, personal loans and education loans. Enter the loan amount, the annual interest rate and the tenure in months to see your monthly EMI, the total interest and the total amount you will repay.

How to use the EMI calculator

  1. Enter the loan amount in rupees.
  2. Enter the annual interest rate quoted by your lender.
  3. Enter the tenure in months. For a 5-year loan, enter 60. For a 20-year loan, enter 240.
  4. Press Calculate EMI to see your monthly payment, total interest and total payment.

EMI formula

The calculator uses the standard reducing balance formula:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

  • P is the loan amount (principal).
  • r is the monthly interest rate: the annual rate divided by 12, then by 100.
  • n is the number of monthly instalments.

Worked example

For a loan of ₹5,00,000 at 8.5% a year for 60 months, r = 8.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.007083 and n = 60. The EMI comes to about ₹10,258. Over 60 months you pay about ₹6,15,496 in total, of which about ₹1,15,496 is interest.

What changes your EMI

  • Loan amount: a larger loan means a larger EMI and more interest.
  • Interest rate: even a small difference in the rate adds up over several years.
  • Tenure: a longer tenure lowers the EMI but raises the total interest. The same ₹5,00,000 at 8.5% over 36 months has an EMI of about ₹15,784 and total interest of about ₹68,216, compared with ₹1,15,496 over 60 months.

Ways to lower the interest you pay

  • Make a larger down payment so you borrow less.
  • Choose the shortest tenure whose EMI you can pay comfortably.
  • Compare rates and fees from several lenders before you sign.
  • Part-prepay the loan when you have spare money. Check the lender's prepayment charges first.
  • Keep your credit score healthy, since lenders often offer better rates to borrowers with a strong record.

Results are estimates for planning. Your lender's figures can differ because of processing fees, insurance, rounding and how interest is counted by day. Check the repayment schedule your lender gives you.

Frequently asked questions

How is EMI calculated?
EMI is calculated with the reducing balance formula shown above. Interest each month is charged on the loan balance that is still outstanding, so the interest part of each EMI falls over time.
Will my bank's EMI match this result exactly?
It should be very close. Banks may differ slightly because of rounding, processing fees, insurance premiums or the way they count days between payments. Use this result for planning and rely on your lender's schedule for the final figures.
Does a longer tenure reduce my EMI?
Yes, the monthly EMI falls when you spread the loan over more months. The trade-off is that you pay interest for longer, so the total interest cost rises.
What is the difference between flat rate and reducing balance?
With a flat rate, interest is charged on the original loan amount for the whole tenure. With a reducing balance rate, interest is charged only on what you still owe. This calculator assumes a reducing balance rate. If a lender quotes a flat rate, the real cost is higher than the number suggests, so ask for the reducing balance rate.
Can I use this calculator for a floating rate loan?
You can use it to estimate the EMI at today's rate. On a floating rate loan, your EMI or tenure changes when the lender changes the rate, so recalculate with the new rate when that happens.
Is the calculation done on my device?
Yes. The calculation runs in your browser when you press Calculate EMI.
