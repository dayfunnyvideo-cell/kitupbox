How to Create a Strong Password (Complete Guide)

3 min read · Nov 12, 2026

Weak and reused passwords are among the most common ways accounts get taken over. A strong password does not have to be hard to live with. This guide covers what makes a password strong, how to build one, and how to keep track of them all.

What makes a password strong

  • Length. Longer is better. Aim for at least 12 characters, and 16 or more for important accounts such as email and banking.
  • Unpredictability. Random characters or random words are hard to guess. Anything based on your life is not.
  • Uniqueness. Every account gets its own password. If one site leaks yours, the others stay safe.

Current guidance from security organizations such as NIST favors length over tricks like swapping letters for symbols. A long random password beats a short one full of special characters.

What to avoid

  • Names, birthdays, pet names, and anything people can find on your social media
  • Common passwords such as "password123" or "qwerty"
  • Keyboard patterns like "1qaz2wsx"
  • Small changes to an old password, such as "Summer2026!" followed by "Summer2027!"
  • Using the same password on more than one site

Two good ways to create a password

1. Use a random password generator

A generator creates random characters that nobody could guess from knowing you. Pick a length of at least 16 and copy the result straight into your password manager or the sign-up form. You do not need to memorize it. With the KitUpBox Password Generator, you open the page in your browser, generate a password and copy it.

2. Build a passphrase

A passphrase is four or more random, unrelated words, such as copper-lantern-orbit-maple. It is long, easy to type and easier to remember. The words must be truly random. A song lyric or a famous quote is not safe.

How to remember so many passwords

  • Use a password manager. Your browser's built-in manager or a dedicated app stores every password for you.
  • Memorize one strong master password. Make it a long passphrase, and do not use it anywhere else.
  • Avoid a plain notes file called "passwords" on your phone or computer.

Turn on two-factor authentication

Two-factor authentication asks for a second proof, such as a code from an authenticator app, when you sign in. Even if someone learns your password, they still cannot get in. Where a service offers passkeys or a security key, those are stronger than text-message codes. Turn it on first for your email, banking and cloud storage accounts.

If a password leaks

  1. Change it right away on that site.
  2. Change it anywhere else you used the same one.
  3. Check whether your email appears in known breaches with a free service such as Have I Been Pwned.
  4. Watch for unusual sign-in alerts for the next few weeks.

Quick checklist

  • 12 or more characters, 16 or more for key accounts
  • A different password for every account
  • A password manager with one strong master password
  • Two-factor authentication turned on
  • No personal details in your passwords

Frequently asked questions

How often should I change my passwords?

Change a password when there is a reason, such as a breach, a shared password or a suspicious sign-in. Security guidance today generally advises against changing strong passwords on a fixed schedule, because forced changes push people toward weaker patterns.

Are passphrases really secure?

Yes, when the words are random and there are at least four of them. A passphrase made from a quote or a sentence about yourself is much weaker.

Is it safe to use an online password generator?

Choose a generator that creates the password in your browser, and read its privacy policy. Never reuse a generated password you pasted into a chat, an email or a document.
